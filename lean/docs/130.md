# Exact Fourier transforms below $`n\log n`$

The following describes the scope of the Lean formalization related to the following accompanying paper(s):

- [An explicit power saving for the exact discrete Fourier transform](../../preprints/An-explicit-power-saving-for-the-exact-discrete-Fourier-transform-September-25-2026/main.pdf)
- [Finite tensor savings and exact Fourier circuits](../../preprints/Finite-tensor-savings-and-exact-Fourier-circuits-September-25-2026/main.pdf)

## Scope

The all-length algorithm in *An explicit power saving for the exact discrete Fourier transform* is not formalized here at this revision. The formalized result below comes from *Finite tensor savings and exact Fourier circuits*: it gives exact discrete Fourier transforms with arbitrarily small normalized circuit cost along an unbounded sequence of lengths. For every $c>0$ and every cutoff $N_0\ge2$, some $n\ge N_0$ has a circuit computing the unnormalized DFT with fewer than $cn\log_2 n$ gates. Addition, subtraction, and multiplication by a predetermined complex scalar each cost one gate; diagonal scalings are charged. The result is subsequential, with no all-length, bounded-coefficient, conditioning, or bit-complexity claim.

The module [PublishedParameters.lean](../OAI/Computability/FourierCircuit/PublishedParameters.lean) checks the displayed $h=100$ finite-network constants by exact integer and rational arithmetic. This certificate does not by itself formalize the network, the all-length recursion, or its running-time bound.

The reusable lemma [RecurrenceDepth.lean](../OAI/Computability/FourierCircuit/RecurrenceDepth.lean) proves a finite-depth bound for a divide-and-conquer recurrence. Instantiating its abstract depth and recurrence hypotheses with the all-length Fourier construction remains future work.

The exploratory [CenterCompression.lean](../OAI/Computability/FourierCircuit/CenterCompression.lean)
proves the characteristic-zero scalar identity behind a proposed removal of the
global centre coordinate: for every three-element target set, subtracting one
third of the global sum from each of the three centre entries gives exactly the
same readout after summing and dividing by two. It also checks the resulting
`h = 24` constants. This is an algebraic certificate only; the frame-labelled
network and its directional-step accounting are not yet formalized.

The accompanying [FrameBudget.lean](../OAI/Computability/FourierCircuit/FrameBudget.lean)
formalizes the arithmetic interface expected from that frame proof: source and
sink dimensions telescope, and every internal decrease contributes twice its
dimension to the absolute residual budget. Its H=24 instance checks the
compressed residual total and saving. The subspace nesting and orthonormal
residual-basis obligations remain explicit hypotheses, so this module does not
claim to complete the binary-frame construction.

The smaller [FrameLabels.lean](../OAI/Computability/FourierCircuit/FrameLabels.lean)
certificate checks the local binary facts used by the side-wire edge: triple
indicator dot products are intersection parities over `ZMod 2`, even
intersection gives line inclusion in the relevant orthogonal complement, and a
three-element indicator line is nondegenerate. Tensor-product labels and the
full eight-row network still require a separate development.

The conditional [SideMergeObstruction.lean](../OAI/Computability/FourierCircuit/SideMergeObstruction.lean)
certificate records the arithmetic consequence of charging two residual units
for every extra incomparable neighbour incidence. At H=24 that penalty is 4176
times the original `2*N0` margin, so such a grouping cannot preserve a positive
saving. The premise is geometric and conditional; it is not a lower bound for
all possible future frame designs.

The [CenterMap.lean](../OAI/Computability/FourierCircuit/CenterMap.lean)
module lifts the three-point identity to the finite set of all 3-subsets. It
proves, first on each source basis vector and then for an arbitrary source
vector, that the compressed centre readout has coefficient
`(|S ∩ T| - 1)/2`. Adding the even-intersection side coefficient gives the
identity map pointwise. Thus the scalar `R G` coefficient matrix is preserved
exactly by the shifted h-coordinate centre map. The frame-labelled invocation
and arbitrary-auxiliary restoration proof remain separate.

The exploratory [SideMomentFactorization.lean](../OAI/Computability/FourierCircuit/SideMomentFactorization.lean)
module proves a second scalar identity: the ordered-neighbour side correction
factors through one global moment and `h` incidence moments, with a direct source
term. It also proves the corresponding add / update / restore cancellation for
one invocation. The identity is exact for arbitrary source vectors, but the
direct source-bank touch and its frame labels are still unproved.

The conditional [SideMomentFrameObstruction.lean](../OAI/Computability/FourierCircuit/SideMomentFrameObstruction.lean)
certificate quantifies the first frame risks: transporting all 25 aggregate
moment wires through a full `m`-dimensional frame costs `8494659993600`, already
3502 times the H=24 saving. Even charging only one h-dimensional varying-factor
drop per moment wire costs `14747673600`, over six times the saving. A viable
moment design therefore needs genuinely shared or lower-dimensional labels; the
scalar factorization alone does not provide that.

The corresponding arithmetic candidate is recorded in
[SideMomentArithmetic.lean](../OAI/Computability/FourierCircuit/SideMomentArithmetic.lean):
with `h=24` it uses 49 auxiliary wires per invocation, gives `W=17185136320`,
`W*=2^35`, and a provisional exponent gap about `5.3552e-7`. These numbers are
research targets only until a reversible frame-compatible schedule is proved.

The [ImprovementComparison.lean](../OAI/Computability/FourierCircuit/ImprovementComparison.lean)
module compares the uncompressed and centre-compressed H=24 multipliers by
exact rational arithmetic. It proves a strict decrease of the recurrence
multiplier by `576081/34359738368`; this is a verified finite-certificate
improvement, pending the remaining frame-labelled circuit proof.

The [JoinPenalty.lean](../OAI/Computability/FourierCircuit/JoinPenalty.lean)
module proves the finite-dimensional geometric core of the side-wire audit: a
common subspace containing two disjoint nonzero lines has dimension at least
two, hence two line-removal charges already cost at least two units. This is a
conditional local obstruction, not a lower bound for every possible circuit.

The [TensorFrameLabels.lean](../OAI/Computability/FourierCircuit/TensorFrameLabels.lean)
module represents the triple tensor address space concretely as functions on a
product of three finite coordinates. It proves pure-tensor dot-product
factorization, unit norm for triple bank entries, and orthogonality whenever a
coordinate pair has even intersection. This establishes the local
nondegenerate line labels used by the neighbour edge; the remaining
tensor-product complement labels and the full eight-row network are still
open.
It now also includes a general pure-tensor dot-product factorization and
orthogonality/unit-norm corollaries for arbitrary local vectors, which are the
tensor-level interfaces used by the transvection residual tools.

The [BinaryTransvection.lean](../OAI/Computability/FourierCircuit/BinaryTransvection.lean)
module adds an explicit characteristic-two orthogonal-coordinate construction.
For an isotropic vector `u`, the map `x ↦ x + (u · x)u` is an involutive
dot-product isometry.  When a triple indicator has a zero coordinate, choosing
`u = t + e_w` maps the coordinate line `e_w` to that indicator.  This is the
local ingredient for constructing orthonormal bases of the residual spaces;
the complete tensor-product stage certificate remains open.
It also proves the two-line analogue: under explicit support-coordinate
hypotheses, a composite of two such transvections identifies the orthogonal
complement of two orthogonal unit vectors with a coordinate hyperplane deleting
two coordinates.  This is the local residual needed for the side row
`P ⊗ ⟨tX,tY⟩⊥`.
The same file now proves the exact direct-sum identity
`⟨y⟩⊥ = ⟨x⟩ ⊔ ⟨x,y⟩⊥` whenever `x` is unit and `x · y = 0`.  This
is the algebraic decomposition required before a two-line residual can be
assembled from independently transported coordinates; it is not yet the full
stage-level labelled-circuit theorem.

The [StageResidualCertificate.lean](../OAI/Computability/FourierCircuit/StageResidualCertificate.lean)
module packages this local decomposition as a reusable certificate interface:
it records a unit source label and an orthogonal target label, exposes the
parent/removed/residual submodules, and supplies a `pure3` constructor from
the tensor-frame lemmas.  It contains no network schedule, operation count,
or all-length recursion theorem; those remain future formalization layers.

The tensor module also exposes generic pure-vector interfaces: a unit pure
tensor spans a nondegenerate line, and a pure tensor whose first factor is
orthogonal to the reference first factor lies in the corresponding reference
line complement.  These lemmas are intended to compose with the two-line
transvection transport above when the remaining complement labels are made
explicit.

The moment obstruction file also records a stronger conditional arithmetic
check.  If one shared moment line is forced to join all `v = 2024` target
triple lines, the resulting `2 I₀ v (h−1)` penalty is exactly
`1,144,222,835,712`, about 472 times the candidate saving.  This premise is
not a universal circuit lower bound; it identifies why a viable improvement
needs a hierarchical or otherwise lower-dimensional aggregation design.

Finally, [TripleSpan.lean](../OAI/Computability/FourierCircuit/TripleSpan.lean)
proves the geometric premise behind that calculation at the indicator level:
for every `h ≥ 4`, all three-subset indicators span `Fin h → ZMod 2`, using
the four-point identity
`e_i = 1_{i,a,b} + 1_{i,a,c} + 1_{i,b,c}`.  In particular the `h = 24`
indicator span is the full 24-dimensional address factor.  This proves only
the span fact; it does not say that every moment network must use one shared
line or establish a circuit lower bound.
The theorem `contains_all_triple_indicators_eq_top` packages the implication
needed by the audit: any submodule containing every triple indicator is already
the full address factor.

The [CenterRowTransition.lean](../OAI/Computability/FourierCircuit/CenterRowTransition.lean)
module now formalizes the generic centre row `P ⊗ D` transition.  For every
unit first-factor label `p`, the fibrewise projection and insertion maps give
an `IsCompl` decomposition of `E ⊗ D` into the parent `B ⊗ D` and a residual
of finrank exactly `h`.  [CenterFrameAudit.lean](../OAI/Computability/FourierCircuit/CenterFrameAudit.lean)
then checks the signed-versus-absolute central cost and the exact role/loss
change from deleting `c_*`.  These are local frame certificates; identifying
all eight paper rows and the complete address schedule remains open.

The concrete [CenterFrameCertificate.lean](../OAI/Computability/FourierCircuit/CenterFrameCertificate.lean)
instantiates a neighbouring tensor-label transition at `h=24`, proving unit
norm, orthogonality, line inclusion, and the two-label residual decomposition.

The exploratory [H18OneStageCandidate.lean](../OAI/Computability/FourierCircuit/H18OneStageCandidate.lean)
records a separate p = 1 one-stage candidate.  Its scalar arithmetic is

\[
h=18,\quad v=N=816,\quad W=409650,\quad W^*=2^{19},\quad
\Delta=168,
\]

with `lambda = 1179627/65536` and `epsilon = 7/393216`.  The scalar terminal
bank map is checked exactly.  This candidate changes the finite network from
the published three-factor construction to one factor; it is therefore not an
improvement theorem for the existing p = 3 network.  A full result still needs
the p = 1 frame labels, nestedness and orthonormal residual bases, the terminal
`U_T^\perp` lift, address correction, and the recursive all-length interface.
The file is an arithmetic certificate and should not be cited as a completed
Fourier circuit construction.

The follow-up [H18TerminalInterface.lean](../OAI/Computability/FourierCircuit/H18TerminalInterface.lean)
audits the standard common-frame terminal bank gate for this candidate.  If
the two roles have endpoint frame dimensions `h` and `h-1`, every common gate
frame incurs
`2*|h-ell| + 2*|(h-1)-ell| >= 2` directional residuals per gate.  Hence the
one-line terminal charge used by the exploratory arithmetic is insufficient
under that interface: at `h=18` the conditional frame-compatible deficit is
`-648`, not `168`.  This is a boundary audit for the proposed construction,
not a universal lower bound for other terminal designs.

For the fixed `h=24` incidence algebra, the reduced centre matrix has rank 24,
so the 24 surviving centre coordinates already meet its factorization rank
bound.  The complementary side matrix `I - RG` is full rank 2024.  These are
exact algebraic boundaries for the current decomposition, not lower bounds for
new routing or frame constructions.

The target-aggregate audit is recorded in
[TargetSideAggregate.lean](../OAI/Computability/FourierCircuit/TargetSideAggregate.lean)
and `ResearchTargetSideAggregateArithmetic.lean`.  Algebraically, one
aggregate per target triple gives an exact reversible scalar update and would
replace `v d_nbr` ordered roles by `v` roles.  Under the existing common-frame
gate and stage-boundary requirements, however, each aggregate needs a
full-frame to `M_S` transition and its inverse.  The exact penalty is `2 I₀ v`;
at `h=24` it is `49,748,818,944`, larger than the centre saving, leaving
effective margin `−47,323,645,952`.  This is a checked obstruction for the
current architecture, not a universal lower bound on new stage boundaries.

The conditional [SideHierarchyArithmetic.lean](../OAI/Computability/FourierCircuit/SideHierarchyArithmetic.lean)
audits a pair-prefix hierarchy intended to make side aggregation local.  For a
fixed pair of coordinates, the triple indicators containing that pair span a
22-dimensional subspace of the 24-dimensional active factor, so a full-frame
excursion and return costs at least four residual units per pair aggregate.
Across the pair aggregates in stages 2 and 3 this is `9045239808`, exceeding
the h=24 saving `2425172992`.  The statement is conditional on the existing
stage boundaries and is not a lower bound for arbitrary side-wire networks.

The moment factorization has also been split into its diagonal-free part in
[SideMomentFactorization.lean](../OAI/Computability/FourierCircuit/SideMomentFactorization.lean).
The theorem `compressed_plus_moment_noDiag` proves that the global and
incidence moments cancel the compressed centre map exactly; the missing
diagonal term is precisely `x S`, so a direct source-to-target bank shear
would have to supply it.  The companion
[SideMomentFrameObstruction.lean](../OAI/Computability/FourierCircuit/SideMomentFrameObstruction.lean)
records the conditional arithmetic consequence: there are exactly
`I₀ v = 24,874,409,472` direct target touches, already more than
the `h=24` centre saving if each direct common-frame gate costs one residual.
This is a conditional gate-cost audit, not a universal obstruction; fusing the
direct term into an existing gate remains an open design question.

The boundary redesign is audited in
[FusedTerminalNoGo.lean](../OAI/Computability/FourierCircuit/FusedTerminalNoGo.lean)
and [TwoShearRotationNoGo.lean](../OAI/Computability/FourierCircuit/TwoShearRotationNoGo.lean).
The first shows that three ordinary restored-auxiliary shears at `h=18` have
deficit `-312`; the second checks all orientations, arbitrary complex
coefficients, and
swap placements and proves that two such shears cannot equal the rotation
matrix.  These results close the naive shear-plus-swap repair while leaving a
 genuinely new non-elementary terminal gate or frame schedule open.

The [PairHierarchyNearMiss.lean](../OAI/Computability/FourierCircuit/PairHierarchyNearMiss.lean)
module stress-tests two more favorable boundary assumptions.  Even if a
pair-prefix aggregate paid only one transition at codimension two, the
penalty would still be `2261309952`, leaving `163863040` saving.  Granting the
stronger combination of one transition and codimension one leaves only
`1294518016`.  Those are fixed-`W* = 2^45` stress-test savings, not exponent
claims for the pair network.  Under the naive pair-role count
`2*N₀ + I₀*(276+h)`, the role count is `20269858048` and the next padding is
`2^35`; the corresponding optimistic numerical gaps would be about
`3.62e-8` and `2.86e-7`, while the unmodified two-stage/codimension-two charge
still leaves a negative effective saving.  A valid positive pair result would
therefore require both this smaller padding and a frame-labelled boundary
schedule; the optimistic variants remain research candidates.  This is a
conditional arithmetic audit, not a universal lower bound.

The [CompressedInvocation.lean](../OAI/Computability/FourierCircuit/CompressedInvocation.lean)
module formalizes the scalar bridge used by a compressed side bank.  Its
`eightRows_restore` theorem proves the eight-row invocation for arbitrary
initial centre and side-bank values, provided the two readout maps satisfy
`R (G x) + J (V x) = x`; no zero-initialization assumption is hidden in the
statement.  The same file proves the three-shear exchange identity and
instantiates the maps for one independent coordinate per legal ordered pair
of triples.  This is an algebraic transition certificate only: it does not
prove frame-label compatibility, physical gate depth, operation counts, or the
all-length DFT recursion.  Those are precisely the interfaces that must be
settled before using the compressed map to claim a new exponent.

The new [GroupedMomentSpan.lean](../OAI/Computability/FourierCircuit/GroupedMomentSpan.lean)
module closes a tempting low-rank variant of the coordinate-moment route.  An
aggregate over a coordinate set `C` touches every triple indicator meeting
`C`.  For `h ≥ 4` and `2 ≤ C.card`, those indicators span the full active
factor, by the four-point identity for each coordinate basis vector.  Thus
simple grouping of two or more incidence moments cannot preserve the
codimension-one labels of singleton moments.  This is a span obstruction for
unweighted coordinate groups, not a lower bound for weighted factorizations,
target-dependent routing, or new stage boundaries.

The exact scalar [ComplementMomentTradeoff.lean](../OAI/Computability/FourierCircuit/ComplementMomentTradeoff.lean)
certificate records the weighted cancellation variant.  If `A = ∑ aᵢ` and
`bᵢ = A - 3*aᵢ`, then `A - bᵢ = 3*aᵢ` and
`A = (∑ bᵢ)/(h-3)`.  A target triple therefore uses `A` and three `bᵢ`
entries.  This is a query-density tradeoff: complement moments may retain
codimension-one source labels, but the global sum is a dense read.  The
certificate is scalar only and does not rule out a weighted framed design.
